Refined RASAero II Benchmark Source Catalog
Executive summary
Starting from the RASAero II comparisons page, I verified a strong public-source benchmark spine for rocket and missile aerodynamics built around NACA RM A53D02, NASA TR R-100, the matched ARCAS pair NASA TN D-4013 and NASA TN D-4014, the contractor Aerobee 150A handbook AST/E1R-13319, NASA TN D-2163 by Suttles, NACA RM L52D15a, NACA RM L53D14a, NACA RM L54D27, NASA TN D-4064, NASA TR R-474, and NACA RM L55G06a. Most of those are on official NTRS pages; two of the RASAero-linked primaries that surfaced cleanly in this tool pass were mirrored PDFs on the RASAero site rather than NTRS search results. [1]
For immediate benchmark construction, the most valuable sources are not all equivalent. NASA TR R-100 is the strongest high-volume official source for zero-lift drag plus geometry metadata across many fin-stabilized bodies. NASA TN D-4013 and NASA TN D-4014 are the strongest matched transonic-to-supersonic static-stability pair in the verified set. NASA TN D-2163 is the strongest whole-vehicle source for component deltas, including fins removed, auxiliary rockets removed, and fairing changes over Mach 0.22–4.65. NACA RM L53D14a is the clearest verified three-fin drag family. NACA RM L54D27 and NASA TN D-4064 are the most useful verified powered-flow sources for base, boattail, and plume interaction. NACA RM L52D15a is the strongest clean component-isolation source for fin planform and fin-body interaction, even though it uses two horizontal fins and is therefore better suited to submodel validation than direct whole-vehicle acceptance testing. [2]
The main gaps after this pass are also clear. Dense, conventional rocket experimental coverage below about Mach 0.22 is still thin in the verified set. In addition, two user-prioritized targets remain on the carry-forward verification queue for a subsequent pass because I did not obtain authoritative browser-accessible source pages or PDFs for them here: NASA TN D-2042 and NACA RM SL55F15. I therefore do not assert their titles, metadata, or ranges below.

In this pass, most high-value sources are still DIGITIZATION_REQUIRED, because the aerodynamic data are presented primarily in plots, even when geometry tables are present. Every row includes a clickable source citation pointing to the PDF or source page.

The matrix shows three practical truths. First, the verified set is already strong from roughly Mach 0.6 to Mach 6, with useful extensions downward to Mach 0.22 through Suttles and upward to Mach 10 through A53D02 for zero-lift drag. Second, the best verified three-fin family in this pass is L53D14a. Third, the best verified powered-flow chain is L54D27 → D-4064, which gives a clean transonic-to-hypersonic plume/base progression. Provenance assessment and unresolved targets
Most of the strongest sources in the catalog are on official NASA/NTRS pages, which is the preferred outcome for provenance and long-term reproducibility. That is true for R-100, D-2163, D-4014, D-4064, R-474, L52D15a, L53D14a, L54D27, L55G06a, TM74058, and TM84599. [49]
Two RASAero-prioritized reports surfaced cleanly in this tool pass as secondary-host mirrors rather than as NTRS search results: A53D02 and D-4013. Because the mirrored PDFs themselves are the original NACA/NASA reports, they remain primary technical content, but the repository host is secondary. D-4014 is slightly different: its official NTRS metadata page is verified, and the page advertises an available PDF download, but the browser preview did not render the PDF during this pass. [50]
For benchmark governance, I would therefore tag sources in four provenance buckets:

Ingestion priority timeline
The sequence below is optimized for the current goal: validate an existing correlation-based solver before broadening the benchmark tree. The first wave focuses on drag baselines; the second wave adds the ARCAS transonic/supersonic crossover; the third wave adds whole-vehicle and component-delta validation; the fourth wave adds powered-flow and roll extensions; the last wave adds meta-sources and failure-envelope coverage. [54]
 

Digitization plan
A practical first pass is to digitize summary figures before dense appendices, and to capture geometry and reference-convention metadata before coefficient points. That order minimizes wasted work: you can immediately start comparing solver outputs against high-value curves while the more labor-intensive configuration-by-configuration extraction proceeds in parallel. For every source, preserve report number, page, figure/table number, axis labels, units, digitizer name, and an estimated digitization uncertainty. [55]
